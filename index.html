Tokyo  ·  June 12 – 16

Four days that moved faster than we could photograph

We landed at Narita and checked into the Andaz Toranomon Hills, and from the first morning the city pulled us in every direction. Hamarikyu Gardens, Asakusa and Senso-ji, a hedgehog cafe, the Imperial Palace grounds and Ueno, an afternoon at teamLab Planets in Toyosu, Akihabara, Ginza — by day four we had stopped trying to see everything and just followed whatever was interesting.

Location data from the trip confirms the shape of it: Saturday was Hamarikyu and Asakusa, Sunday was the Imperial Palace and a long walk toward Shibuya, Monday was teamLab followed by Akihabara and Ginza, and Tuesday we barely left the neighborhood.

Jun 13 — Hamarikyu & Asakusa

Our first full day. We walked Hamarikyu Gardens in the morning, then took the bus up to Asakusa for Senso-ji and Nakamise-dori — and somewhere in there, a hedgehog cafe that the kids still bring up.

Jun 14 — Imperial Palace & west Tokyo

A long walking day: the Imperial Palace East Gardens in the morning, Ueno in the late morning, then a slow drift west toward Shibuya as the afternoon wore on.

Jun 15 — teamLab Planets, Akihabara, Ginza

teamLab Planets in Toyosu took up most of the late morning — the wading-through-light rooms, the mirrored infinity space, the kids soaked to the knees and delighted about it. Akihabara and Ginza filled out the rest of the day.

Jun 16 — a slow day

After three full days, we let this one be quiet. Mostly the neighborhood around the hotel.

Tokyo, early in the trip

Hakone  ·  June 17

A long drive, a lake, and a night in a ryokan

We left Tokyo in the late morning and didn't arrive until early afternoon — Hakone is close on a map and further in traffic. Once there: Hakone Shrine and the red torii at the edge of Lake Ashi, a slow cruise across the water, and a ryokan for the night with a kaiseki dinner that the kids are still talking about.

Dinner as ceremony

The kaiseki arrived course by course, each on its own lacquerware. Nobody complained about any of it — not even the parts none of us could name.

Kyoto  ·  June 18 – 20

The city that asks for nothing but your attention

The drive down from Hakone took most of the day; we arrived at the machiya in the mid-afternoon and spent the evening around Nishiki Market and Gion, working off the ride. The next two days had a rhythm to them — mornings out, long stretches on foot through the same few blocks near Nishiki and Teramachi, an evening trip to Fushimi Inari that turned out to be shorter than any of us expected.

Jun 18 — Arrival, Nishiki Market, Gion

We checked into the machiya in the afternoon and spent the rest of daylight wandering Nishiki Market and the streets around Gion.

Jun 19 — Kyoto center, Fushimi Inari

Most of the day was spent around the Teramachi arcade and central Kyoto. In the evening we made it out to Fushimi Inari — a short visit, the light already going amber on the gates.

Jun 20 — back to the same blocks

We barely moved this day — over an hour spent within 150 meters of the same spot near Nishiki, just browsing. Dinner in the evening, then a long walk back.

Kyoto, evening

Nara & Arashiyama  ·  June 21

The biggest single day of the whole trip

This was the day we covered the most ground — a drive out to Nara in the morning (just over an hour there, deer and all), back into Kyoto for Kinkaku-ji, and then out again to Arashiyama for the bamboo grove and the river in the late afternoon. Three very different places, all before dinner.

Deer, gold, and bamboo

Nara's deer are not shy about it — they will take a cracker straight out of your hand and act like they're owed one. Kinkaku-ji in the early afternoon light, gold against the pond, was the postcard moment of the whole trip. And Arashiyama at the end of the day — the bamboo grove, the rickshaw through it — felt like the right way to close out the biggest day.

Arashiyama, bamboo grove

Tokyo, once more  ·  June 22 – 23

The last night, from higher up

Shinkansen back into Tokyo, then the Park Hyatt in Shinjuku for the final night — the same hotel from Lost in Translation, which felt fitting after twelve days of being pleasantly disoriented in the best possible way. We spent the evening on the rooftop, then Shinjuku the next morning before the flight home: Shinjuku Chuo Park, the Metropolitan Government Building observation deck, and a last walk through Shinjuku Gyoen.

Coming back around

Twelve days earlier we'd landed not knowing quite what to expect. The last night, looking out over the city from up high, it was hard to believe how much had happened in between. The flight home was quiet — the good kind of quiet, everyone turning the trip over in their heads.
